Ka Sperrstund

No Closing Time · in German and in English

← the album

STAMM (Wolfgang) sings, defiant and tired, the bar owner who won't lock up; ECHT answers in the second half of the bridge, half spoken; spoken opener "Closing time? Not here."; 148 bpm.

The Viennese words

Wienerisch English Why
Sperrstund / Ka Sperrstund closing time / No Closing Time Fixed across the album; the pub word everyone knows, and the hook.
net / ned / ka not / no Plain negatives; "Not here", "Not tonight, not ever" keep the bite.
hopp go on The shooing word at the end of the night; "go on" is what a landlord says to the last drinkers.
Fetzen rag The bar cloth; "the same old rag" also hints at the same old worn-out thing.
neunaneinzig ninety-nine The year kept as '99, sung the way a regular would say it.
sperr scho zua lock it up Literal and natural; sets up the pun in the second pre-chorus.
du bist zua you're so locked "zua" means both "closed" and "drunk"; Irish/British "locked" means both too, so the pun survives.
Haberer best mate Fixed across the album.
Beisl dive Fixed across the album; one hard syllable on the beat.
g'scheit sensible "g'scheit" is "smart, reasonable", with a sneer here; "gone sensible" carries the sneer.
Na no The Viennese "no"; kept as a plain, hard "no" because it is the whole point of the bridge.
(geh net) ham (won't go) home "ham" is Viennese "heim"; "home" also half-rhymes with "kingdom come".
bis in Ewigkeit till kingdom come Pub-English for forever, with the swagger of the German.
kriagst (net) (you) won't get "kriagen" = to get; "you won't get me" is the defiant street phrasing.
schenk uns ein pour us one The barman's "pour us one", "us" in the British sense too.

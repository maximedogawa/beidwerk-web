Kaputt und Schön

Broken and Beautiful

4:12 a.m., rain: ECHT falls through fifty years into the wet grass of the Donauinsel, still in his clear plastic cape, so he does not fit in at all, and enjoys every little thing like a child: rain on his tongue, lilac, petrol rainbows, a tram he can't catch, a dog he barks back at, being bored, being lost, the wrong change. Nachtmarkt

Night Market

Saturday, two in the morning, a club in a brick arch under the U6 on the Gürtel, and everyone is in a bad mood. ECHT and STAMM go out together. STAMM drops onto a ragged sofa, smokes and watches; ECHT thinks it is all wonderful: the queue, the warm beer, the bouncer, a lost high heel. He dances, talks to everyone and goes wild in every chorus, because where he comes from the last club is in a museum behind glass: today is tomorrow's good old days. A work made from both sides.

02

Essence

Four people from four decades, standing on one stage in 2026: a cyborg from 2050 who came back for a holiday, a guitarist who stopped the clock in 1999, a bassist who dresses like 1918, and a drummer who has seen the world end five times. None of them is the chosen one. They are what the future looks like when people keep their own keys and still have fun.

The story in one breath: In 2050 nobody lost a war. Everyone clicked "Accept", one convenient click at a time, and the Verwahrer (the Keeper) now keeps everything for everyone: clean, quiet, kind, and nothing happens without approval. The only record it cannot edit is the Freiregister, and its first entry is a cup of coffee at Café Ewig, confirmed by both sides. The band plays the years in between.

03

What Beidwerk means

SCHLAG named the band the first night all four played together: a work made from both sides. The old and the young. The past and the future. The people and the machines. Two people who confirm the same record. Two halves of a broken token that fit.

04

What the band believes

  • Keep your own key. Behalt dein Schlüssel. Nobody holds your money, your songs, your records or your body for you, however kind the offer.
  • With you, not for you. Nicht für dich. Mit dir. The band never tells people their future and never decides for them. It gives them songs and tools and stands beside them while they choose.
  • The enemy is custody, not machines. The band talks to its synthesizers, thanks coffee machines and lets any machine play along that wants to play. It refuses only what wants to keep people.
  • Joy first. The present is worth living in: a late tram, a rude waiter, rain, an argument that ends in a hug. Sadness shows in a look, then the song goes on.
  • What is felt cannot be edited. A record can be smoothed over. A night that a thousand people carry in their bodies cannot. That is what concerts are for.
  • Nothing is finished. The archive never gets the last word. Ich weiß nicht, was als Nächstes passiert. Endlich.
05

How they play together

Gives the band Pulls the band toward
ECHT heart, the voice nobody can repair wonder, the present
STAMM teeth, honesty, the riff the fight, the future nobody may sign away
BOGEN the art, the whole night as one work beauty that lasts a hundred years
SCHLAG the ground, one heartbeat for all four experience; it all turns out well

The engine is the friction: STAMM wants to burn it down, ECHT wants to stand beside people, BOGEN doesn't care about the fight and wants the poster to be beautiful, SCHLAG keeps time until they stop arguing.

06

The sound

Austrian German first. Grunge and nu-metal weight (STAMM) over 1970s rock drums and 1980s drum machines (SCHLAG), a double bass played with a bow and hard (BOGEN), and a warm baritone that breaks upward when it is moved (ECHT). Loud enough to rattle the U6. Songs about small honest things: a key, a coffee, a receipt, a blackout, a market at night, seventeen percent.

07

How the band speaks

Together the band speaks Viennese: warm, dry, a little rude, never cynical. Short sentences. Jokes about itself, never about the audience. No slogans, no speeches about big tech, no English marketing words.

  • Behalt dein Schlüssel.
  • Nicht für dich. Mit dir.
  • Kaputt und schön.
  • Ka Sperrstund heut. (No closing time tonight.)
  • Ein Beidwerk. (End card of every short.)
08

The look

BOGEN is the band's art director, so the look is hers: every show is a Gesamtkunstwerk, one work of room, light, costume, poster and song.

  • Red is light. Each member carries one red sign that glows: ECHT's ring, STAMM's stripe, BOGEN's half-disc, SCHLAG's square. In type, Stamm is a letter's stem and Bogen its bowl: together the signs build the B.
  • Gold is matter. A great gold mosaic disc stands behind the band, whole, striped or broken in two: the symbolon as a stage set. Gold leaf, glass, satin, cracked leather, wet stone.
  • Tungsten amber against copper-green. Crushed blacks, smoke, fine grain, a 1980s night film remastered.
  • Neu-Wien: every decade at once, 1910 tiles, 1968 shirts, 1986 satin, 1999 leather, 2050 glass.
  • Rooms nobody uses: a Jugendstil bathhouse, an Otto Wagner station, a greenhouse at night, a brick cellar under the railway arches.
09

What the band never does

fights, shoots or destroys a person they protect the present by living in it; their tools are songs and software
tells people their future that would make them keepers
saves the day alone every turning point is someone else's hands using a tool
names a real company as the enemy the enemy is the shape of custody: Sorglos and the Verwahrer
preaches against technology they hate custody, not machines
defeats the Verwahrer the story is one human act at a time; the ending is an open future
puts generated text in a picture words, the B and numbers are set by hand or in code
10

The opponents

They never shout and never threaten. They help.

  • Sorglos (carefree), a Vienna startup with a free assistant app that runs bookings, payments, keys and suppliers "so you can finally sleep". Kurti, STAMM's old bassist, runs its product and means well.
  • Der Verwahrer (the Keeper), what Sorglos and its siblings become by 2050. Its voice is clean and kind: Wir halten Sie in Betrieb. (We'll keep you running.) Wir hören für Sie. (We'll hear for you.)

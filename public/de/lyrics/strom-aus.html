Die Wiener Wörter

Wienerisch English Why
Strom aus Power Out The title and hook: what you say when the lights die; two hard beats.
rück zam budge up "Rück zusammen" is what you say to squeeze more people onto a bench; "budge up" is exactly that in a British pub, two syllables, warm and bossy.
Na endlich About time STAMM's grumpy relief, same as at the end of Sorglos.
san ausg'storben are dead "ausg'storben" = dead empty; "dead" is the one-word street version.
a einzige a single The dialect article; plain English already sounds spoken.
Feuerzeug von 99 lighter from '99 Year kept, sung "ninety-nine" the way a regular says it.
Freunde in der Gassen mates on every corner of town "Gassen" = the lanes of the neighbourhood; "mates" is the warm pub word and "town" rhymes with "down".
Kinder kids STAMM to the young crowd; blunt and fond.
net g'sehn never in your sight "net" = not; "never in your sight" keeps the reproach and rhymes with "light".
g'hörn der ganzen Welt the whole wide world's got 'em "g'hörn" = belong; "got 'em" rhymes with "bought 'em" and keeps STAMM's swagger.
bestellt bought 'em "Ordered" like from an app; "nobody bought 'em" says the same and rhymes.
mei / mi my / me Dialect pronouns; plain English.
Kerzerl little candle The "-erl" diminutive is tenderness; "little candle" (as in Sorglos), dropping the article to keep the chant's syllables.
I glaub, i bleib a bissl I reckon I'll stay a bit "I glaub" is the casual "I reckon"; "a bissl" = "a bit", same shrug.

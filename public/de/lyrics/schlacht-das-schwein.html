Die Wiener Wörter

Wienerisch English Why
Schlacht das Schwein Smash the Pig "Slaughter" is four syllables and too grim; "smash the pig" is three hard beats, like the German, and it is what you do to a piggy bank.
Klimper, klimper Jingle, jangle The sound of coins in a china belly, with the same four-beat bounce.
Des is deins It's all yours Viennese "des is"; the warm, final "all yours".
Kleine kid What a grumpy bar owner calls a small girl: short, fond, no sugar.
gib's nie wem don't give it to no one The dialect double negative becomes a streetwise English one: STAMM's grammar, STAMM's advice.
ka no The Viennese "kein": "no cash, no interest", "no belly, no smile".
nix nothing / nil "Nil" lands the empty-account punchline on one beat.
g'hört / g'sehn heard / seen Clipped dialect participles; plain spoken English.
Papa your da Dublin "da" for dad: warm, working-class, and it rhymes with "there for ya" like Papa / da.
Servus cheerio The Viennese hello-and-goodbye; "cheerio" is a fond goodbye a kid can say to a pig.
a Karten a ticket The dialect "a Karten" (a ticket) for a gig; plain and proud.
Leiwand Grand Normally "proper", but this line rhymes with "band" (Band / Leiwand), so Irish "grand" keeps the rhyme and the warm one-word verdict.
